The following analysis examines two Tableau Public visualizations representing data from the United Nations MY World 2030 survey. Both aim to communicate public perceptions and progress toward the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), with a particular emphasis on six goals identified as areas of immediate concern. While both visualizations use UN data, they differ significantly in how effectively they communicate information and engage viewers.

The first visualization demonstrates several weaknesses that limit its effectiveness. Although it utilizes the UN’s official icons and presents accurate data, the lack of clear labels and an overwhelming mix of visual elements stacked blocks, line charts, and icons creates unnecessary cognitive load for viewers. The result is a cluttered and confusing dashboard that obscures the intended message. 
Users must click on each icon to understand its meaning, which interrupts the flow of information and reduces accessibility. Moreover, filters such as “High Human Development (HDI group)” are poorly labeled, requiring prior knowledge or extra effort to interpret. Despite containing meaningful data, the visualization fails to guide the audience clearly or aesthetically.

In contrast, the second visualization effectively applies data visualization principles that enhance clarity and user engagement. It features clearly labeled icons, concise summaries, and intuitive filters that make it easy for users regardless of their familiarity with the SDGs to explore the data. The inclusion of maps helps display regional trends, while supporting bar graphs provide structured comparisons without adding visual clutter. These thoughtful design choices reduce cognitive strain, promote comprehension, and result in a more aesthetically balanced and purposeful presentation.

    - How could this data visualization have been improved?

      The poor dashboard could be improved by simplifying its layout and separating each chart types. Replacing the combined stacked-block and line chart with a single, more appropriate bar or categorical chart would reduce extraneous cognitive load (Sweller, 1988) and highlight key comparisons. Each UN goal icon should include text labels. Filters like HDI group could have short explanations to help the user understand the information.

      The good visualization could be enhanced by adding short annotations that summarize key insights . This would improve overall interpretation. Additionally, ensuring a more neutral color palettes would make the dashboard accessible to a wider audience.
