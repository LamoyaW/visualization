## Assignment 3: Final Project

### Requirements:
- We will finish this class by giving you the chance to use what you have learned in a practical context, by creating data visualizations from raw data. 
- Choose a dataset of interest from the [City of Toronto’s Open Data Portal](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/open-data/) or [Ontario’s Open Data Catalogue](https://data.ontario.ca/). 
- Using Python and one other data visualization software (Excel or free alternative, Tableau Public, any other tool you prefer), create two distinct visualizations from your dataset of choice.  
- For each visualization, describe and justify: 
    > What software did you use to create your data visualization?
    Python and Power BI

    > Who is your intended audience? 
    Business leaders
    
    > What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 
    The visulaization highlights the top five industries in ONtario by productivity in 2024 and shows their performance over the past decade. The goal is to demonstrate how industries have changed over the over the 10 years which could be helpful for business leaders to understand which sectors offer stable returns or investment opportunities. 
    
    > What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 
    In creating this visualization, I applied three core qualities of effective data visualization: aesthetic clarity, substantive depth, and perceptual accessibility.
    - Aesthetic clarity: I used a clean layout with simple color choices and minimal visual clutter. This ensures the chart remains readable and professional, especially for business audiences who value clarity over decoration.
    - Substantive depth: By including 10 years of productivity data (2014–2024), the visualization goes beyond short-term fluctuations and captures long-term industry trends. This allows viewers to assess performance before, during, and after the pandemic, offering a more robust basis for strategic insight.
    - Perceptual accessibility: Labels, axis titles, and legends are clearly defined and aligned with the audience’s level of understanding. The message is easy to interpret without requiring technical expertise, making it suitable for executive decision-makers.
    I also followed four key conventions of data visualization:
    - Two-dimensional layout for intuitive comparison
    - Geometric shapes and lines to represent trends
    - Clean structure with consistent spacing and alignment
    - Source attribution included directly in the image for transparency and credibility
    Together, these design choices ensure the visualization is not only informative but also trustworthy and easy to engage with.

    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 
    I set a random seed for the visualization in python for reproducibility. For power BI any changes in the underlining data will lead to a change in the visualization. 

    > How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible? 
    I ensured accessibility by using color only to enhance data interpretation, so the visualization remains clear even without color. I also selected Arial at 12 pt or larger for legibility across devices and audiences.
    
    > Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  
    This visualization could impact business leaders, policymakers, and economic analysts who rely on productivity trends to guide investment, workforce, and infrastructure decisions. 

    > How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 
    I included the productivity values from 2014 to 2024 to highlight long-term industry trends and ensure the visualization captured both pre- and post-pandemic shifts. 

    > What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?
The final visualization is supported by significant underwater labour, including data cleaning, column renaming, and formatting to ensure consistency across a decade of records. It also reflects the invisible contributions of those who collected, maintained, and published the original dataset, as well as the design and accessibility choices made to serve different audiences. .
